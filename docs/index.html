Origin Lens reads an image's C2PA Content Credentials, verifies their signature on your device, and opens every result with a color-coded verdict. EXIF metadata and the edit history are shown alongside. Yellow means there is nothing to verify, not that an image is fake.

A color-coded verdict for every image

New in 1.4.2 on the App Store. The color follows a fixed order: red over purple over green over yellow.

  • Green: Content Credentials Valid. The signature is valid and the image is unchanged since signing. The signer is not checked against a trust list, and the result says so.
  • Purple: AI-Generated (Signed). Valid, signed credentials declare the image as AI-generated.
  • Red: Credentials Invalid or Credentials Don't Match This Image. The credentials fail validation, or the image was changed after it was signed.
  • Yellow: nothing to verify. No Content Credentials, a screenshot, credentials held elsewhere, an expired certificate, or a signer that could not be validated.

Most images carry no Content Credentials at all, so yellow alone is not a sign of manipulation.

FAQ

What is Origin Lens?

Origin Lens helps you check where a digital image comes from. On your device, it reads C2PA Content Credentials, verifies their signature, and checks whether the image was changed after signing. An optional reverse image search shows where else the image has appeared online, as context for your own judgment.

What is C2PA?

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) is an open technical standard that allows publishers to embed tamper-evident information about who created a piece of content and how it was altered. Origin Lens reads this data and shows what the credentials record about how the image was made and edited.

What does a yellow verdict mean?

That there is nothing Origin Lens can verify: the image has no Content Credentials, is a screenshot, points to credentials elsewhere, or its certificate is expired or could not be validated. Most images carry no credentials at all, so yellow alone is not a sign of manipulation.

Why might an AI image not be detected?

Most AI tools do not embed C2PA metadata yet. Taking a screenshot instead of downloading removes all metadata, social media platforms strip C2PA on upload, and many image editors remove it.

How do I test C2PA detection?

Download an image from the public C2PA test files (github.com/c2pa-org/public-testfiles), save it to the Files app (not Photos), and open it in Origin Lens with "Select from Files". The result shows what the file's Content Credentials record. Some test files are deliberately broken, so a red result can be correct.

What leaves my device?

Nothing during verification: C2PA and EXIF analysis run on your device. Three optional features use the network, and each asks first. The AI opinion sends the image to the Google Gemini API. Reverse image search sends the image, or its web address, to the services named in the prompt (imgbb and SerpAPI, or Google, Bing and Yandex directly). Fetching credentials held elsewhere contacts the host the image names, but does not send your image.
